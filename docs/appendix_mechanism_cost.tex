\section{Cost of Cross-Type Eviction}
\label{app:cost}

Allowing either resource to evict the other adds work a fixed partition
does not do: an incoming expert needs a contiguous super-block, so warm KV
pages in its way are physically relocated. We measure that cost to
establish it is not a hidden tax.

Byte movement is timed with CUDA events on the pool's transfer stream, in
the configuration of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} (one NVIDIA L4; expert
slot 12\,MiB; $F=96$ pages of 128\,KiB), over 500 repetitions.

The expert load is bounded by PCIe and is paid by any expert-offloading
system. A single expert load is worth $8.4$ relocations, so preserving warm
KV state is cheap relative to the expert transfer that displaces it.

Aggregate cost stays small because relocation is infrequent. Across the
KV$\rightarrow$expert shift of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} the pool
performed roughly $21{,}000$ relocations, about $2.4$\,s of copying, or
$0.1\%$ of that run.\footnote{Provisional: scaled from a traced run with a
shorter expert phase; to be replaced by a directly profiled run.} Since
the reported speedup is end-to-end wall-clock time with the mechanism
active, this cost is already contained in it rather than additional to it.

These figures cover byte movement, which includes the GPU-side copies.
Host-side victim selection is excluded; it is a comparison over per-layer
expert recency and global prefix recency, answered from one end of an
ordered structure, and is flat in pool occupancy.
