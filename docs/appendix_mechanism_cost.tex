\section{Cost of the Unified Mechanism}
\label{app:cost}

Making KV pages and expert weights interchangeable adds work that a fixed
partition does not do: the pool must choose which resident item loses, and
an incoming expert may have to relocate warm KV pages to assemble a
contiguous super-block. This appendix measures both, to establish that
neither is a significant tax relative to the transfer they schedule.

We separate two components. \emph{Byte movement} is timed with CUDA events
on the pool's transfer stream. \emph{Policy} is the host-side work of
selecting a victim and clearing a super-block, timed with a wall clock.
Both are measured in the configuration reported in
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}: one NVIDIA L4, OLMoE-1B-7B-Instruct, 16 MoE
layers, an expert slot of 12\,MiB, and $M=67$ super-blocks of $F=96$ pages
of 128\,KiB (6432 pages). Byte-movement figures are means over 200
repetitions; policy figures are means over 20, and are reported against
\emph{KV occupancy}, the fraction of the pool holding evictable
cached-prefix pages.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Byte movement in the unified pool. One page relocation moves the
page in every layer's buffer, because a KV block is global.}
\label{tab:cost-bytes}
\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r r}
\toprule
\textbf{Operation} & \textbf{Mean} & \textbf{p99} & \textbf{Bytes} & \textbf{Rate} \\
\midrule
Expert load (pinned host $\rightarrow$ pool) & 0.946\,ms & 0.947\,ms & 12\,MiB & 12.4\,GiB/s \\
One page relocation ($16\times$128\,KiB) & 0.116\,ms & 0.195\,ms & 2\,MiB & 16.8\,GiB/s \\
\quad same bytes, one contiguous copy & 0.012\,ms & 0.016\,ms & 2\,MiB & 160.3\,GiB/s \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The expert load is bandwidth-bound on PCIe and is the irreducible term:
any expert-offloading system pays it. Relocation, the operation unique to
the unified pool, costs $0.116$\,ms per page, so one expert load is worth
$8.2$ relocations. Preserving warm KV state is therefore inexpensive
relative to the expert transfer that motivates it.

Resolving an expert miss costs $38\,\mu$s at 95\% KV occupancy, about 4\%
of the 0.946\,ms transfer it schedules, and the cost is flat in occupancy.
Flatness matters more than the absolute figure: the pool does its
cross-type reasoning precisely when memory is scarce, so a policy whose
cost grew with occupancy would be most expensive exactly when it is used
most. Keeping cached-prefix pages in a recency-ordered structure, and
keeping per-super-block state, makes each query answerable from one end of
that order rather than by scanning the pool.

Relocation is limited by kernel-launch overhead rather than bandwidth: a
page relocation issues one small copy per layer and costs $9.5\times$ a
single contiguous copy of the same bytes (Table~\ref{tab:cost-bytes}).
Its latency is consequently flat below roughly 512\,KiB pages
($0.109$\,ms at $F=24$ versus $0.108$\,ms at the $F=96$ we use), while
larger pages achieve better effective bandwidth
($117$\,GiB/s at $F=6$). Fine pages therefore buy KV-side allocation
granularity at no latency cost, which is why we page at the smallest
granularity the fused kernel permits.

Two caveats. The policy measurements drive the real allocator with the GPU
seams stubbed, so they capture host-side cost only. They also place no
pinned or in-use pages in the pool, which removes the early exits a live
forward would take; the figures are therefore upper bounds.
